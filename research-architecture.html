Professional Vision in the Architecture Design Studio

This research examines how students learn to see, describe, and evaluate design problems through participation in architecture studio critiques. Drawing on ethnographic discourse analysis and interaction analysis, I investigate how professional vision emerges through talk, gesture, embodied action, and material artifacts.

Research Questions

  • How do architecture students learn to see like designers?
  • How do critiques socialize students into disciplinary ways of knowing?
  • How do gesture, language, and material artifacts work together during design discussions?

Why It Matters

Professional vision is not simply an individual cognitive achievement. It develops through interaction with instructors, peers, and disciplinary tools. This project demonstrates how learning becomes visible through everyday communicative practices.

Current Status

Current analyses draw on a corpus of 177 architecture critiques, 48 fieldnotes, and longitudinal interactional data following focal students across a semester of studio participation.
