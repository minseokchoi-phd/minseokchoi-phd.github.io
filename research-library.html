Professional Vision in Library Storytime

This emerging line of research examines how public libraries can become more equitable and responsive learning spaces for multilingual children and families. Building on my work in multilingual family literacy and professional vision, I investigate how librarians learn to notice, interpret, and support diverse forms of participation.

Research Questions

  • How do librarians recognize multilingual family competence?
  • What forms of participation become visible during storytime?
  • How can library programs be designed to support equitable engagement?
  • How does professional vision develop among youth services librarians?

Why Libraries?

Libraries are often among the most accessible educational institutions in a community. They serve children and families across linguistic, cultural, and socioeconomic differences, making them important sites for studying participation, belonging, and literacy learning.

Current Projects

Current work focuses on multilingual family learning in public library settings, storytime interactions, and the development of professional vision among library staff. This research extends earlier studies conducted in museums and teacher education settings.

Future Directions

At the University of Illinois School of Information Sciences, I am developing a research agenda that connects multilingual family learning, public libraries, youth services, and humanizing approaches to literacy and information access.
