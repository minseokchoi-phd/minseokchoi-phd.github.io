Finding Books Together

Children, Caregivers, and Librarians in Everyday Library Encounters

Proposal in preparation
Institute of Museum and Library Services (IMLS)
Laura Bush 21st Century Librarian Program · Early Career Research

Status as of October 26, 2026.

Finding a book is also a process of understanding one another. A child's interest may take shape through a gesture, a correction, a caregiver's explanation, or recognition of a book that feels right. This proposed study asks how young children, caregivers, and librarians shape assistance together in everyday book-finding encounters.

The Research Focus

The project examines how children's contributions influence a question, search, or recommendation. It attends to how participants establish what a child is looking for, respond when an initial understanding does not fit, and recognize possible next steps.

The Proposed Approach

Contextual fieldwork and microethnographic discourse analysis will connect the organization of library services with the detail of everyday interaction. Analysis will trace talk, gesture, material resources, and the sequence of responses within whole encounters.

Learning from Encounters

The proposed work will develop case-based learning resources that invite children's services staff to examine possible responses and compare what follows. These resources are intended to support professional reflection grounded in children's and caregivers' participation.

Connections Across My Research

This direction builds on my studies of multilingual family learning in science museums, professional vision, and family learning observation in teacher education. It brings those commitments to children's information practices and everyday assistance in public libraries.
