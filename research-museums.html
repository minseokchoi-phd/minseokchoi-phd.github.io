Multilingual Family Learning in Science Museums

Museum texts and signage shape how multilingual families access, interpret, and participate in learning opportunities.

This line of research examines how multilingual children and families make meaning together during visits to science museums. Using ethnographic discourse analysis and interaction analysis, I investigate how language, gesture, gaze, movement, and material artifacts work together during family learning.

Overview

Museums are often described as open and inclusive learning environments. Yet participation is shaped by institutional texts, exhibit design, language expectations, and assumptions about who museums are for. My research explores how multilingual families navigate these opportunities and constraints while engaging in collaborative meaning-making.

Across museum visits, families draw on rich communicative repertoires that include multiple languages, translation, gesture, pointing, embodied movement, prior experiences, and cultural knowledge. These practices often remain invisible within traditional accounts of literacy and learning.

Research Questions

  • How do multilingual families collaboratively make sense of scientific ideas in museums?
  • How do children and caregivers use language, gesture, gaze, and material artifacts during museum learning?
  • How do museum texts and signage support or constrain multilingual participation?
  • How do multilingual families negotiate institutional expectations while maintaining their own communicative practices?

Why This Work Matters

This research contributes to broader conversations about equity, access, and representation in public educational institutions. Rather than viewing multilingualism as a barrier, I examine how multilingual practices function as intellectual resources for learning, participation, and family engagement.

By documenting the sophisticated ways families interact with museum environments, this work challenges deficit-oriented assumptions and highlights possibilities for more inclusive educational design.

Current Directions

Current work extends this research beyond museums into public libraries and other community learning environments. I am particularly interested in how institutions can develop professional vision for recognizing multilingual family competence and designing more equitable opportunities for participation.
