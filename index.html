About

I am an Assistant Professor in the School of Information Sciences at the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign.

My research explores how children, families, and educators learn across homes, schools, libraries, museums, and other community settings. I am particularly interested in how multilingual children and families draw on diverse linguistic, cultural, and semiotic resources to make meaning and participate in literacy practices.

Drawing on ethnographic, discourse analytic, and multimodal approaches, I study multilingual family learning, translanguaging, early literacies, and teacher education. Across my work, I seek to develop more equitable and humanizing approaches to learning by recognizing the strengths and knowledge that children, families, and communities bring to educational settings.

Research

Early Literacies Across Contexts

I investigate how young children develop literacy practices across homes, libraries, museums, and schools, with particular attention to informal and community-based learning environments.

Multilingual Family Learning

My research examines how multilingual families engage in literacy, language, and meaning-making practices through everyday interactions, family activities, and participation in community spaces.

Translanguaging and Multimodal Meaning-Making

I study how children and families use language, gesture, material objects, movement, and other semiotic resources to communicate and learn across languages.

Teacher Education and Professional Learning

I explore how preservice teachers learn to recognize and build upon the strengths of multilingual children, families, and communities through community-engaged and inquiry-based teacher preparation.
