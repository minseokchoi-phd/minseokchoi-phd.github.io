About

I am an Assistant Professor in the School of Information Sciences at the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign.

My research examines how children, families, and educators learn across homes, schools, libraries, museums, and other community settings. I am particularly interested in how multilingual children and families draw on linguistic, cultural, and semiotic resources to participate in literacy practices and learning.

Drawing on ethnographic, discourse analytic, and multimodal approaches, I investigate multilingual family learning, early literacies, translanguaging, and teacher learning across formal and informal educational contexts.

My work seeks to advance equitable and humanizing approaches to literacy and learning by recognizing the strengths, knowledge, and experiences that children, families, and communities bring to educational spaces.

Research

Early Literacies Across Contexts

I study how young children develop literacy practices across homes, schools, libraries, museums, and community spaces.

Multilingual Families and Communities

I examine how multilingual families engage in literacy, language, and meaning-making through everyday interactions and participation in community life.

Translanguaging and Multimodal Meaning-Making

I investigate how language, gesture, movement, material objects, and other semiotic resources support learning across languages and settings.

Teacher Learning and Professional Development

I explore how educators learn to recognize and build upon the strengths of multilingual children, families, and communities.
