My scholarship and everyday life unfold together. As a father, educator, multilingual family member, and researcher, I keep notes on moments that make me pause.

These entries are not research findings. They are records of experiences, relationships, and questions that continue to shape what I notice and carry into my scholarship.

Field Museum · Chicago

Pokémon, fossils, and a return to SUE

At the Field Museum, Pokémon offered my children a familiar path into the unfamiliar world of fossils. Characters they already knew became invitations to look more closely, compare forms, and ask how creatures from different times might be understood.

We also returned to SUE. Seeing my children’s excitement on meeting this familiar presence again reminded me that learning is shaped not only by new encounters, but also by revisiting places and objects that have become part of a family’s shared memory.

Griffin Museum of Science and Industry · Chicago

A story traveling across languages

At the Griffin Museum of Science and Industry, we encountered Anne Frank’s story through editions of her diary in many languages. A Korean edition rested among the books. My children stayed with the story with a concentration that surprised me.

Watching them, I thought about how a young person’s words travel across languages, generations, and places—and how literacy can make a distant history present within a family’s shared experience.
